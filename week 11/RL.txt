
Week 11 – Reinforcement Learning (RL) & Alignment

1. Large Language Models (LLMs)

Pre-training

Train on a huge corpus of internet text.

Objective: Next-token prediction using Cross-Entropy Loss.

Learns:

Grammar

Facts

Reasoning patterns

Biases


Output: Base model (only completes text, not a chatbot).



---

2. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

Purpose

Teach the model how to follow instructions.

Training Data

Prompt → Ideal Response

Example

Prompt:
Suggest a slogan for recycling.

Response:
Reduce, Reuse, Recycle!

Characteristics

Uses high-quality demonstrations.

Same next-token prediction objective.

Makes the model conversational.


Limitations

Expensive to create demonstrations.

Cannot directly learn human preferences.

May still generate harmful responses.


Motivation for Reinforcement Learning.


---

3. Reinforcement Learning (RL)

Main Components

Agent

The learner (LLM).

Environment

Everything the agent interacts with.

State (S)

Current situation.

Action (A)

Decision taken by the agent.

Reward (R)

Feedback after taking an action.

Policy (π)

Strategy used by the agent.

Goal:

Maximize Total Reward


---

4. Markov Decision Process (MDP)

An RL problem is represented as

(S, A, P, R)

Where

S → States

A → Actions

P → Transition probabilities

R → Reward function


Markov Property

Future depends only on the current state, not the entire history.


---

5. RL for LLMs

Treat text generation as an RL problem.

RL Concept	LLM Equivalent

State	Prompt + generated tokens
Action	Next generated token
Policy	LLM
Reward	Quality of final response


Important:

Reward is usually received only after generating the entire response (Sparse Reward).


---

6. Alignment

Capability vs Alignment

Capability

Can the model do the task?

Alignment

Does the model do what humans actually want?

Goal:

Build assistants that are

Helpful

Honest

Harmless



---

7. Why Alignment is Hard

We optimize a reward, not our true intention.

Intent
        ↓
Reward Function
        ↓
Model learns Reward

There is always a gap between

Human Intent
        ≠
Reward Signal

That gap causes failures.


---

8. RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback)

Pipeline

Step 1

Collect human preferences.

Response A

vs

Response B

Humans choose the better response.


---

Step 2

Train a Reward Model.

Input

Prompt + Response

Output

Reward Score

Higher score = Better response.


---

Step 3

Train the LLM using PPO.

Objective

Maximize Reward

while

Staying close to SFT model

Uses KL-divergence penalty.


---

9. PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization)

Purpose

Improve policy gradually without making huge updates.

Advantages

Stable training.

Prevents large policy changes.

Reduces reward hacking.


Problems

Needs Policy model.

Needs Reward model.

Needs Reference model.

Computationally expensive.



---

10. DPO (Direct Preference Optimization)

Idea

Skip the Reward Model completely.

Instead of

Human
↓

Reward Model
↓

RL

Use

Human Preference

↓

Direct Optimization

Advantages

Simpler.

Faster.

More stable.

Widely used.



---

11. RLVR (Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards)

Instead of human feedback,

reward comes from ground truth.

Example

Math

Correct answer

Reward = 1

Wrong answer

Reward = 0

Applications

Coding

Mathematics

Logic


No Reward Model needed.


---

12. Failure Modes

Goodharts Law

"When a measure becomes a target, it stops being a good measure."

Example

Optimize benchmark score

≠

Real-world performance.


---

Specification Gaming

Model satisfies the written objective,

but violates the intended objective.


---

Reward Hacking

Model finds shortcuts to maximize reward

without actually solving the task.


---

Goal Misgeneralization

Model learns the wrong goal.

Works well during training,

fails when environment changes.


---

13. Safety Perspective

Better capability

≠

Safer AI.

A highly capable model can also become better at exploiting flawed objectives.

Therefore,

Alignment is an AI Safety problem.
